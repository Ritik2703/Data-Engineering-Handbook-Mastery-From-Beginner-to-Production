# 1. The Interview Process — What to Actually Expect

## The Typical Data Engineering Interview Pipeline (product/tech companies)
```
1. RESUME SCREEN (recruiter or ATS) — 30 seconds to 2 minutes of actual
   human attention, if it gets past automated keyword filtering at all

2. RECRUITER SCREEN (15-30 min, phone/video) — basic fit check: are
   your skills/experience roughly aligned, salary expectations, notice
   period, work authorization

3. TECHNICAL SCREEN (45-60 min, often with a senior IC, not the
   hiring manager) — usually SQL + Python live coding, sometimes a
   quick system design warm-up question

4. ONSITE / VIRTUAL ONSITE LOOP (3-5 rounds, often same day or spread
   across a week):
   - SQL/coding round (deeper than the screen)
   - System design round (see module 11, applied live)
   - Behavioral round (see file 3)
   - "Bar raiser" / culture-fit round (varies by company)
   - Sometimes a take-home assignment REPLACES one or more of the
     above, reviewed in a follow-up discussion round

5. HIRING MANAGER / TEAM FIT (often a less formal conversation, but
   genuinely evaluative — "would I want to work with this person")

6. REFERENCE CHECKS + OFFER
```
**Real variation**: startups often COMPRESS this (fewer, more informal rounds); large enterprises often EXTEND it (more rounds, more formal rubrics, sometimes a separate "values" interview) — always ask the recruiter directly what THEIR specific process looks like rather than assuming.

## What Each Stage Is ACTUALLY Testing (beyond the surface topic)
```
Recruiter screen: mostly LOGISTICS fit, but also basic COMMUNICATION —
  can you explain your background clearly and concisely? A rambling,
  unfocused answer here can genuinely end the process early, regardless
  of technical skill.

Technical screen (SQL/Python): NOT just "do you know the syntax" — also
  testing whether you can work through a problem OUT LOUD, handle being
  slightly stuck without freezing, and communicate your reasoning as you go.

System design round: testing JUDGMENT (module 11) far more than
  encyclopedic tool knowledge — the interviewer usually cares MORE about
  your requirements-gathering and tradeoff reasoning than whether you
  picked the exact tool they had in mind.

Behavioral round: testing whether you'll be a GOOD TEAMMATE and whether
  your past behavior predicts good future behavior in THIS specific
  role/team — genuine self-awareness and honest reflection score far
  better than a rehearsed "perfect" story with no real learning shown.

"Bar raiser" / senior-level rounds: often SPECIFICALLY testing whether
  you'd raise the technical/cultural bar of the team, not just meet the
  minimum — expect deeper "why" follow-up questions probing your
  actual understanding, not just your ability to recite a concept.
```

## How Long This Actually Takes (set realistic expectations)
```
From first application to signed offer: commonly 3-8 WEEKS at a
  reasonably responsive company, sometimes considerably longer at
  large enterprises with more approval layers — genuinely useful to
  know so you don't panic or assume rejection from normal process delay.
```

## Remote/Virtual Interview-Specific Practical Tips
```
- Test your screen-share/whiteboard tool BEFORE the interview starts,
  not during it
- Have a notepad/scratch document ready for system design sketching if
  no shared whiteboard tool is provided
- Genuinely OK to ask "can you repeat/clarify the question" — much
  better than answering the wrong question confidently
```

## Interview Traps
- Don't treat the recruiter screen as unimportant "just logistics" — genuine communication clarity here sets the tone for how the rest of your candidacy is perceived internally before you even reach a technical round.
- If you don't know what a specific company's process looks like, ASK the recruiter directly — this is a completely normal, expected question, not a sign of weakness.
