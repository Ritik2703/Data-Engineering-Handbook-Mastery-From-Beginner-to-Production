# 10. Common Mistakes & Red Flags — From Both Sides of the Table

## Candidate Mistakes That Actually Fail Interviews (compiled patterns)

### Technical Rounds
```
- Jumping straight to code/a solution without clarifying the problem
  first (recap file 4/5's emphasis on restating/clarifying before coding)
- Going completely silent while working through a problem, leaving the
  interviewer unable to follow your reasoning or offer a helpful nudge
- Presenting a solution as finished without testing it against even
  a simple example first
- Bluffing confidently about something you don't actually know, rather
  than honestly reasoning from related knowledge (recap file 4's
  guidance on this)
- Ignoring edge cases entirely unless EXPLICITLY prompted for them
```

### System Design Rounds
```
- Skipping requirements clarification and diving straight into "we'll
  use Kafka + Spark + Snowflake" (recap module 11 file 1's junior-vs-
  senior comparison — this is THE most common way candidates read as
  junior regardless of actual seniority)
- Presenting a design with NO explicit tradeoffs mentioned, as if the
  chosen approach has no cost at all (recap module 11 file 8)
- Defending an initial design rigidly when the interviewer pushes back
  with new information, rather than genuinely re-reasoning (recap
  module 11 file 10's pushback-handling guidance)
- Reflexively choosing the most sophisticated/trendy technology without
  justifying it against the STATED requirements
```

### Behavioral Rounds
```
- Vague, non-specific answers with no concrete situation described
  (recap file 3)
- A "failure" story that's secretly a humblebrag with no genuine
  learning shown
- Badmouthing a previous employer/manager — even if genuinely justified,
  this reads poorly and raises concern about how you'd speak about
  THIS company later
- No prepared questions when asked "do you have any questions for us"
  — signals a lack of genuine engagement/curiosity
```

### Process/Communication
```
- Being unresponsive/slow to schedule or reply during the process
  itself (a real, if informal, signal recruiters/hiring managers
  genuinely factor in)
- Not following up with a brief, genuine thank-you note after
  interviews — small, but a real, low-cost positive signal
- Being dishonest about experience/skills that get exposed by a
  follow-up technical question — far more damaging than honestly
  saying "I haven't worked with that specifically"
```

## Red Flags to Watch For as a CANDIDATE Evaluating the Company (this cuts both ways)
```
- Interviewers who can't clearly articulate what the team is currently
  working on or why the role is open (signals internal disorganization)
- Evasiveness about on-call expectations, team turnover, or why the
  previous person in this role left
- A consistently disorganized, repeatedly-rescheduled interview process
- Pressure tactics (unreasonably short offer deadlines with no
  legitimate reason given)
- Job posting/interview descriptions of the role that shift
  significantly and inconsistently across different interviewers you
  speak with (signals the role/team may not have a clear, agreed
  mandate internally)
```

## The Single Biggest Meta-Mistake (across every round type)
```
Treating an interview as a TEST TO PASS rather than a GENUINE TWO-WAY
CONVERSATION to determine mutual fit. Candidates who genuinely engage
with curiosity (asking real questions, showing authentic interest in
HOW a company solves problems, being honestly reflective in behavioral
answers) consistently outperform candidates treating it as a
memorized-answer performance — this shows up subtly across EVERY
round type covered in this module, and interviewers, even without
being able to fully articulate why, consistently rate genuine
engagement higher than rehearsed perfection.
```

## Interview Traps
- If you notice yourself making one of these candidate mistakes DURING an actual interview (e.g., realizing you've gone silent for too long, or that you're defending a position too rigidly), it's genuinely fine to self-correct in the moment ("let me back up and think through this differently") — self-awareness demonstrated LIVE is itself a strong, positive signal, not an admission of failure.
