# 8. Negotiation & Offer Evaluation

## Evaluating an Offer — Beyond Just the Base Salary Number
```
Total compensation components to actually consider:
- Base salary
- Bonus (target %, and REALISTIC historical payout — ask directly)
- Equity/stock (RSUs at a public company have a fairly clear value;
  options/equity at a private company/startup are genuinely
  speculative — understand vesting schedules and realistic outcomes)
- Benefits (health insurance quality, retirement matching, genuinely
  varies a lot and has real financial value)
- Signing bonus (often used to bridge a gap, sometimes with a
  clawback if you leave within a certain period — read the fine print)

Non-compensation factors genuinely worth weighing:
- Learning/growth trajectory (will this role build genuinely
  valuable, current skills — recap this repo's emphasis on staying
  current with evolving tools)
- Team/manager quality (arguably the single biggest factor in
  day-to-day job satisfaction, hard to assess but worth genuinely
  trying to via questions asked during the process)
- Company financial health/stability (especially relevant for
  smaller companies/startups)
- Work-life balance/on-call expectations (recap `08-orchestration/08`'s
  on-call reality discussion — worth asking directly about this for
  a DE role specifically)
```

## How to Actually Negotiate (most candidates under-negotiate)
```
The single most important fact: MOST companies EXPECT some negotiation
and build a small amount of room into their initial offer — simply
ASKING (professionally) is far more likely to improve your offer than
NOT asking, and rarely backfires if done reasonably and respectfully.

A simple, effective negotiation approach:
"Thank you so much for the offer, I'm genuinely excited about this
role. Based on [my experience/market research/a competing offer, if
genuinely true], I was hoping we could explore [a specific number or
range] — is there flexibility here?"

Key principles:
- Be SPECIFIC (a number or range), not vague ("can you do better?")
- Have GENUINE justification ready (market research, a competing
  offer, specific relevant experience) — recruiters respond better to
  reasoned asks than pure assertion
- Negotiate the WHOLE package, not just base salary, if base has
  limited flexibility (sometimes signing bonus/equity/start date has
  more room to move)
- NEVER lie about a competing offer that doesn't exist — this is a
  genuinely risky, sometimes discoverable, and ethically poor practice
```

## Comparing Multiple Offers (a genuinely common, good problem to have)
```
Beyond raw compensation, weigh:
- Which role builds skills most aligned with where you want your
  career to go in 2-3 years (recap module 11's senior/architect
  career-progression discussion)?
- Which team/manager did you get the STRONGEST genuine positive signal
  from during the process?
- Which company's technical stack/maturity genuinely excites you to
  work in daily (recap this repo's coverage of legacy vs modern stacks
  — some candidates genuinely prefer greenfield modern-stack work,
  others prefer the stability/scale of enterprise legacy environments)?
```

## Red Flags to Watch For During the Process Itself (this is data too)
```
- A disorganized, unresponsive, or repeatedly-rescheduled interview
  process can be a genuine signal about internal team dysfunction
- Vague or evasive answers when YOU ask about on-call expectations,
  team turnover, or why the role is open
- Pressure to accept an offer with an unreasonably short deadline,
  without a genuine reason given
```

## Interview Traps
- "What are your salary expectations?" (often asked EARLY, by the
  recruiter) — research market rates beforehand (levels.fyi, Glassdoor,
  and genuinely talking to people in similar roles) so you give a
  grounded, confident range rather than an uninformed guess or an
  overly-hesitant non-answer.
- Never accept an offer on the spot in the excitement of the moment if
  you have ANY genuine hesitation — it's completely normal and expected
  to ask for a few days to consider, even after a strong, exciting offer.
